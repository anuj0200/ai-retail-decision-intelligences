# AI-Powered Retail Decision Intelligence

An end-to-end analytics project for a fictional retail chain, **Bharat Retail Co.** (20 stores across India). It goes from raw sales data to forecasts, customer groups, an AI-written monthly summary and an interactive dashboard, and it ends with recommendations a manager can act on.

> **Note:** all data is synthetic (generated by `generate_retail_data.py`). It was built to contain realistic business patterns, so the findings show the method, not a real company.

## What makes this project different: the AI is checked, not trusted
Most AI demos let the model write everything. I tested that, and it failed: the first versions got a number's direction wrong ("profit decreased" when it increased) and invented a business "target" that did not exist.

So I split the job:
- **Python writes every sentence with a number,** so figures and "up" or "down" are always exact.
- **A local AI model (Llama 3.2 via Ollama) writes only the Risk and Action lines.**
- **Automatic checks** reject output that contains numbers, invented words or the wrong format.
- **A human reviews the final text.** AI drafts, people decide.

The AI also runs locally, so no business data leaves the machine.

## The business question
Sales are growing, but the company doesn't know where it loses money, which customers it is losing, or what to stock. This project answers those questions with data.

## Key findings
1. **The data is clean,** with no missing values or broken links. 2,584 sales lines (2.8%) were sold below cost, and about 94% of them happened on promo days. Grocery caused most of them. The loss is small (about Rs 40,000), but the pattern repeats on every promo.
2. **Revenue grew 12.7%** (Rs 2.26 crore in 2024 to Rs 2.54 crore in 2025). Festive months (October and November) spike every year, but the October 2025 record in revenue came with flat profit, because the margin fell to about 28%.
3. **Grocery brings about 10% of revenue but only about 2.5% of profit** (margin 8.5%, against 46.6% for Apparel). The best-selling products are cheap items with thin margins. Large stores bring about 53% of revenue.
4. **One third of customers (Champions) bring 68% of revenue.** 818 "At Risk" customers, who spent about Rs 91 lakh in total, have stopped buying. About 19% of buyers purchased only once.
5. **Forecast:** a Random Forest model beat a simple baseline (daily error about 20% vs 33%) and was accurate within about 2% on whole months. It predicts about **Rs 22.8 lakh for January 2026** (planning range plus or minus 5%).
6. **Surprises:** one store (Jaipur) had no sales for 11 days in August 2024, and one snack product sold 8 times its normal volume in March 2025. Sales gaps alone can't reliably show stockouts (a test flagged about 2,000 gaps and only about 35 were real), so daily inventory data is needed.
7. **In 2025 the margin slipped** from 35.3% to 34.9% while revenue grew, which fits the heavier festive promotions.

## Recommendations
- Cap promo discounts on Grocery below its margin.
- Run a win-back campaign for the 818 At Risk customers, and reward Champions with perks instead of discounts.
- Set alerts: a store with no sales for more than 7 days, and a product above 4 times its normal weekly units.
- Plan January 2026 stock for about Rs 21.7 to 23.9 lakh of sales.
- Collect daily stock levels, so stockouts can be detected and not guessed.

## The AI layer
A monthly CEO summary is built in two parts. **Python writes every sentence that contains a number**, so figures and "up" or "down" are always exact. **A local AI model writes only the Risk and Action lines**, then automatic checks look for stray numbers, invented words and the wrong format. Earlier versions where the AI wrote everything made mistakes (a wrong direction, an invented "target"), so a human still reviews the final text.

## Limitations
- The data is synthetic and covers only two years.
- No inventory data, so stockouts can't be measured.
- The forecast assumes the Republic Day sale repeats on 20 to 28 January.
- The free local AI model is small and needs human review.
- Customer groups are based on both years together, so they look different when the dashboard is filtered by year.
